# UK personal finance app market: competitor analysis

**Prepared:** 16 August 2026
**Scope:** B2C (consumer) surface only. Nothing here applies to `/for-business`.
**Method:** live pricing pages, official help centres, vendor community forums, review aggregators. All prices verified against the vendor's own site where one exists. Sources listed at the end.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Five things matter more than the rest of this document.

1. **The aggregation market is consolidating fast, and it is consolidating towards free.** Money Dashboard closed in October 2023. Moneyhub's consumer app shut on **14 August 2026, two days ago**, deleting user data. Meanwhile Monzo now sells account aggregation, custom categories and an auto-spreadsheet export for **£3 a month** inside the bank people already use, and Snoop gives away account linking, categorisation, subscription tracking, bill reminders, contract renewal reminders and a credit score for **£0 forever**. Competing on "we track your money more cheaply" is competing against zero.

2. **Emma does not yet have recurring income detection, and it is their single most requested missing feature.** Emma's CEO has been promising it in their own community since 2020, said in May 2026 that an engineer is building it, and said on 11 June 2026 that it is still in testing. Users in that thread say verbatim that it is "the one feature missing that makes me go looking in the App Store for an alternative app to Emma." This is a real, dated, documented gap and it is exactly what the founder wants to mirror.

3. **Paybacker's actual differentiator has a direct, cheaper, faster-moving competitor that the founder has not named: NoReply (usenoreply.com).** AI complaint letters with UK legal citations, evidence analysis, ombudsman finder, delivery and open tracking, replies routed back into the case, 67 free SEO tools, a browser extension, an iOS app in progress. Pricing: free tier, £9.99 per case, or **£29 a year unlimited** (£2.42/month) and £49/year for six people. That is roughly half Paybacker Essential for the letter use case. This is the competitor to benchmark against, not Emma.

4. **Paybacker's true unique assets are the dispute state machine, the outcome dataset, email inbox scanning and MCP.** No money app does disputes. No dispute tool has bank or email data. Paybacker is the only product sitting on both sides, and that combination, not the price, is the story.

5. **The pricing premise is half right and strategically weak.** Paybacker does bundle for less than the sum of the parts, but nobody buys the parts. The honest read is in section 6.

## 2. Competitor detail

### Emma (emma-app.com)
UK, Emma Technologies Ltd, FCA registered, ~3 million customers. 4.7 on the App Store (24,000+ reviews), 4.3 on Google Play (8,800+ reviews). The market leader for UK aggregation-plus-budgeting.

**Pricing (verified on the compare page, 16 Aug 2026), 7-day free trial, 30% off annual:**

| Tier | Monthly | Annual | Bank logins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | £0 | £0 | 2 |
| Plus | £4.99 | £41.99 (£3.49/mo eq.) | 4 |
| Pro | £9.99 | £83.99 (£6.99/mo eq.) | Unlimited |
| Ultimate | £14.99 | £124.99 (£10.49/mo eq.) | Unlimited |

Extra members on Ultimate: up to 4 people at £5.99/month each.

**Gating that matters:** budgeting and enhanced tracking, cashback, credit score, fraud detection, priority support and web app all start at Plus. Net worth, custom categories, CSV import and data export start at Pro. Credit alerts, Spaces, business accounts, NordVPN are Ultimate only. Basic is deliberately thin, it is a demo.

**Does well:** categorisation accuracy, recurring payment and subscription detection, breadth (savings pots, investing, ISA/JISA, rent reporting to all three bureaux on Pro), a real community forum where the CEO answers, and aggressive land-grabbing of displaced Moneyhub users with a published migration guide.

**Complaints:** Trustpilot skews heavily towards billing. Recurring themes are charges after free trials without clear notice, confusion between App Store billing and Emma's own direct billing, difficulty cancelling, and slow or unhelpful support on refunds. Product-wise, the loudest gap is recurring income (see section 3).

### Snoop (snoop.app)
Owned by Vanquis Banking Group since July 2023. 1.2m+ installs. Trustpilot around 3.2.

**Pricing:** Snoop free, forever. **Snoop Plus £5.99/month or £47.99/year.**

**Free includes:** all accounts in one app, daily balance updates, automatic categorisation, monthly and all-time spend analysis, spend by merchant or category, weekly spending report, monthly and category budgets, regular payment and subscription tracking, **weekly reminder of upcoming bills**, **contract renewal reminders**, exclusive switching deals, daily personalised money-saving "Snoops", credit score tracking, savings account.

**Plus adds:** unlimited custom categories, manual accounts, net worth, transaction export, payday-to-payday tracking, bills by pay cycle, unlimited custom spending reports, unlimited spending alerts, **unlimited refund alerts** (track a refund you are expecting, Snoop tells you if it does not arrive).

**Does well:** the most generous free tier in the UK market by a distance, and price-hike detection on regular payments. This is the benchmark Paybacker's free tier is actually judged against.

**Complaints:** confusing data presentation, open banking connections that work for one account and fail for others, no mortgage account support, being logged out, slow savings deposits and withdrawals, and general nervousness about handing bank credentials to a lesser-known brand.

### Plum (withplum.com)
Savings and investing first, budgeting second. Restructured tiers on 7 July 2025.

**Pricing:** Basic £0, **Plus £3.99**, **Boost £7.99**, **Max £14.99** per month. Legacy Pro/Ultra/Premium tiers persist for pre-July-2025 subscribers.

**Does well:** automated round-ups and rules-based auto-saving, Cash ISA / LISA / SIPP / GIA under one roof, declining AUM fee by tier (0.60% down to 0.15%), and a lifestyle perks bundle on Max (travel insurance, ClassPass, cinema, NordVPN).

**Complaints:** Trustpilot is dominated by money-movement failures. Withdrawals not arriving, ISA transfers out taking months against a 2-day benchmark elsewhere, refunds over 35 working days, AI-generated support replies with no human escalation path, app glitches affecting investments and login.

**Relevance to Paybacker:** low direct overlap. Plum is a savings product. It is only in the comparison because it takes the same £4 to £15 wallet share.

### Cleo (meetcleo.com)
AI chat-first money assistant, UK-founded but now US-revenue-led.

**Pricing:** free tier plus paid. UK: **Cleo Plus £5.99/month**, **Cleo Pro £12.99/month**. US: Plus $5.99, Pro $8.99, Builder $14.99 (or $134.99/year). UK feature set is materially narrower: **no credit, lending, savings or interest-bearing products in the UK.** Cleo Pro UK gives Early Income, up to £300 of earned-but-unpaid salary for a £0.99 admin fee.

**Does well:** genuinely good conversational UX and personality-led retention. This is the closest analogue to Paybacker's Pocket Agent and worth studying for tone, though Cleo has no dispute capability whatsoever.

**Complaints and risk flag:** the FTC alleged Cleo advertised access to hundreds of dollars of instant advances while most users received far less and were charged extra for same-day access, and that cancellation was made difficult. **$17 million settlement** going to consumer refunds. Trustpilot is broadly positive (~80% five star across 3,300+) but recent reviews are mixed on advance eligibility and support latency.

### Moneyhub (moneyhub.com) — CLOSED
Was £1.49/month or £14.99/year, no free tier, six-month trial. Closed to new customers in 2025. **Consumer app shut on 14 August 2026**, after which users cannot log in and data is permanently deleted. Management of the app was handed to WPS Advisory as Moneyhub pivoted fully to enterprise open banking. Its loyal base valued it for pensions, investments, property (Zoopla) and manual assets in one net worth number, which is a different job from spend tracking. **These users are in market right now and nobody has fully replaced the net-worth-across-everything job.**

### Money Dashboard — CLOSED
Neon and Classic both shut on **31 October 2023**, all accounts closed, bank connections severed, data deleted or anonymised. Acquired by ClearScore in early 2022, which could not find a sustainable consumer model and pivoted to B2B. Its "Forecast" feature is still the thing ex-users ask for by name, which is more evidence for the forward cash-flow build.

### Monzo and Starling (built-in features)
**Monzo (Aug 2026):** free account, then **Extra £3/month**, **Perks £9/month**, **Max from £19/month** (3-month minimum, +£4/mo family). Extra is the dangerous one: it bundles **connected banks and credit cards** (aggregation across most major UK banks), **custom categories**, **auto-spreadsheet** (automatic export to a sheet), virtual cards, advanced round-ups and two credit scores with weekly updates. Free Monzo gets one connected account and one credit score.

Forward view: **Left to Spend** in Trends is current balance minus upcoming scheduled payments and set budgets, plus an estimated balance. Bill Pots ring-fence direct debits so they are excluded from Left to Spend. Salary Sorter splits pay across pots on arrival. Monzo Payday shows salary early. Billsback randomly refunds bills up to £150 for paid tiers.

**Starling:** free only, no paid personal tiers. Spaces, Bills Manager (pay direct debits straight from a Space), income allocation to Spaces with an end-of-month projection, virtual cards. No per-category budget limits.

**Why this matters:** the two banks most likely to be a Paybacker user's main account already ship a decent forward-looking budgeting view, and Monzo now sells aggregation and sheet export for £3. Aggregation is not a moat, it is a cost centre.

### "Lunchflow" — what the founder likely means
Two distinct things, both worth knowing.

**Lunch Money (lunchmoney.app)** is a US-built, indie, web-first budgeting tool with a devoted power-user following. Pricing changed in 2026: **pay-what-you-want annual with a $60/year minimum from 15 March 2026** (early subscribers locked at $50/year for life), a $10/month option, ranges up to $150/year, and **no feature tiers at all, everything included at every price**. Plaid-based sync (~20,000 institutions), ML categorisation, rules engine, **recurring items including income**, rollover budgets, multi-currency (160+), net worth.

**Lunch Flow (lunchflow.app)** is a separate third-party bridge built by a Lunch Money user. It uses PSD2 open banking across 40+ countries including the UK to push daily transactions and balances into Lunch Money, Google Sheets, Actual Budget, Sure and Firefly III. It exists because Lunch Money's own UK bank coverage is weak.

**Read:** if the founder said "lunchflow", he almost certainly means the Lunch Money ecosystem, and the transferable ideas are (a) one price, no feature tiers, no upgrade friction, (b) recurring items covering income and expenses as a single first-class concept, and (c) treating "sync my money into other tools" as a feature. Point (c) is already Paybacker's MCP and Sheets export, which is closer to Lunch Flow's positioning than anything Emma or Snoop does.

### NoReply (usenoreply.com) — the competitor that actually matters
UK AI consumer-advocacy platform. Not a money app, a dispute app. Directly attacks Paybacker's core claim.

**Pricing:** Free (full analysis, what you are owed and which law says so, case strength, escalation route, 3 evidence files, all 67 free tools, 0 written complaints). **Single case £9.99** (premium AI, video and image analysis, 5 files, send from NoReply). **Pro £29/year** (£2.42/month, unlimited cases, 20 files, delivery and open tracking, replies land back in your case, follow-up reminders, priority support). **Pro Family £49/year for 6 people.** You keep 100% of any payout.

**Why this is serious:** it does AI letters with legal citations, company research, evidence analysis, deadline reminders, escalation paths and an ombudsman finder. It has 67 free no-signup tools (flight compensation calculator, Section 75 and chargeback checker, refund timeline checker, cancel subscription generator, GDPR request, small claims calculator), per-company landing pages for BA, Ryanair, easyJet, O2, BT, British Gas, EE and Amazon, and published complaint-index data pages. That is a textbook programmatic SEO moat aimed at exactly Paybacker's intent keywords. A browser extension ships now, an iOS app is listed as coming.

**Where it is weaker:** no bank connection, no email inbox scanning, no subscription tracking, no automatic detection of problems. Every case has to be initiated by the user, who must already know they have a complaint. Paybacker's email and bank scanning finds the dispute for you. That is the wedge.

**Also in the dispute lane:** Resolver (free, generic templates, escalation paths, complaint tracking, 5m+ users), Which? (free guides, £10.75/month membership for full templates), Citizens Advice (free templates and an interactive letter tool). And the cautionary tale: the FTC's finalised order against **DoNotPay** (February 2025, $193,000 monetary relief, notice to 2021 to 2023 subscribers) prohibiting "AI lawyer" claims that were not substantiated by testing against human-lawyer output. Marketing language is a compliance surface.

## 3. The feature the founder wants to mirror: Emma's upcoming payments view

### What Emma actually does today

**1. Recurring payments detection.** Emma detects recurring payments automatically from direct debits, standing orders and repeated card purchases, and files them into a Recurring payments section in the Feed tab. Users can also add one manually, and can edit the predicted price, predicted payment date, frequency, name and expected min/max amount ("Edit under Next Payment").

**2. Committed Spending.** Feed tab, tap the Budget circle chart, then Committed Spending. It is defined as "your upcoming payments for the current period" and is the set of payments Emma predicts will happen inside your budgeting period. It is sourced entirely from the Recurring payments list. Credit card repayments and internal transfers are auto-excluded so they do not distort analytics. Committed Spending is what drives "how much you have left to spend".

**3. True Balance.** Available balance minus all committed spend on that account for the period. Emma's own worked example: £1,200 in the account, a £1,000 bill due in a week, True Balance £200.

**4. Payday.** Set your pay cycle and Emma adds a feed card counting down days to the next salary, plus a "current payday" period in Analytics so all budgeting runs payday-to-payday rather than calendar month. Supported cycles: weekly, fortnightly, four-weekly, fixed date 1st to 28th, last working day, and dynamic rules like "last Thursday of the month". Emma maintains a UK public holiday database and shifts the predicted date when payday falls on a weekend or bank holiday. Past paydays and multiple paydays are explicitly not supported.

**5. Proactive warning.** Reviewers consistently single out the alert that fires when Emma believes an upcoming subscription payment will push the account negative before next payday.

### The critical finding: Emma does NOT do incoming money

Emma's prediction engine is **outgoings only**. There is no recurring income object. This has been an open request in Emma's own community since April 2020, and the trail is damning:

- **Apr 2020,** Emma staff: "We don't currently have the functionality to assign income as a reoccurring."
- **Aug 2023,** user: "In analytics we have regular payments, why not recurring income? The need is for forecasting."
- **Mar 2025,** CEO Edoardo Moreni: "we don't have it yet; but this has been requested and will be built." Later the same month: "at this stage, it's not in the pipeline... I can see it will improve the true balance, but that's about it."
- **Nov 2025,** user: "Surely this has to be basic functionality for any budgeting app? How can you possibly manage cash flow if you are only permitted to predict outgoings?" CEO: "you are right! We have this feature designed and we should prioritise it soon; I don't have an exact ETA."
- **Feb 2026,** user: "This is the number one feature I am waiting for in Emma."
- **May 2026,** user: "This is the one feature missing that makes me go looking in the App Store for an alternative app to Emma." CEO, 3 May 2026: "We have an engineer working on improving the detection of recurring payments; as part of this work, we are introducing regular income detection. This will be reflected in the recurring payments list as well as the true balance."
- **30 May 2026,** CEO: "we have also included positive payments which we'll rollout at a later stage."
- **11 June 2026,** CEO: "We need to wait a bit longer for this, but the work is in place, we are just testing everything."

Concrete user pain quoted in that thread: four-weekly income means Emma fires false overdraft alerts mid-month because it cannot see the incoming payment, and Emma's auto-transfer-to-pots algorithm under-saves for the same reason.

**So: as of 16 August 2026 Emma's incoming-money view is either just rolling out or still in test, six years after first being asked for.** If the founder has seen Thursday showing Friday's incoming payment in Emma, he has caught an early rollout. Either way this is a fresh, narrow, well-documented window.

### How the rest of the market handles it

| Product | Upcoming outgoings | Upcoming incoming |
|---|---|---|
| Emma | Committed Spending + True Balance, strong | Not shipped as of mid-2026, in test |
| Snoop | Weekly upcoming-bills reminder (free), bills by pay cycle (Plus) | No income forecast |
| Monzo | Left to Spend = balance minus upcoming scheduled payments and budgets; Bill Pots; estimated balance in Trends | Payday early access, no general income prediction |
| Starling | Bills Manager pays direct debits from a Space | Allocate income to Spaces, shows projected month end, manual |
| Lunch Money | Recurring items | **Recurring items include income**, first-class |
| PocketSmith | Calendar of scheduled expenses | **Calendar of scheduled income, projected daily balance**, the best-in-class forecaster (roughly £9.99/mo billed yearly) |
| Cleo | Light | Early Income advance, not a forecast |

**Design implication for Paybacker.** Do not copy Committed Spending. Copy the job. The right primitive is one **recurring cash-flow item** with a sign, a predicted date, a predicted amount, a confidence, a frequency and a bank-holiday shift rule, detected from Yapily credits as well as debits. Then render one screen that answers the question a person actually has on a Thursday night: **what is landing, what is leaving, and what is left**, projected to the next payday and to month end, with a red line where the balance would go negative. Emma's public roadmap tells you the exact acceptance criteria, and their community thread gives you the marketing copy for free.

## 5. Gap analysis

### (a) What competitors have that Paybacker lacks

**Table stakes, build these:**
1. **Recurring income detection and forward cash-flow projection.** Everyone's job-to-be-done, nobody in the UK does it well, Emma is mid-rollout. Highest priority.
2. **Native mobile app, or at minimum an installable PWA with push notifications.** Every serious competitor is app-first. Budgeting is a phone habit and the App Store is the distribution channel. Push also fixes the 90-day open banking re-consent cliff that silently kills every aggregator's retention.
3. **Manual and offline accounts plus manual assets.** Snoop Plus and Emma Pro both have it. Without it you cannot do credible net worth, and you cannot serve the Moneyhub refugees who are in market this month.
4. **CSV import.** Emma Pro has it. It is the migration on-ramp for every user leaving a closing app.
5. **Payday-cycle budgeting periods.** Emma has supported weekly, fortnightly, four-weekly, fixed-date, last-working-day and dynamic rules since 2018, with bank-holiday shifting. Calendar months alone look amateur.
6. **Custom categories and a rules engine.** Emma Pro, Snoop Plus, Monzo Extra, Lunch Money.
7. **Credit score.** Free on Snoop, free on Monzo, Plus on Emma. Commodity, but its absence loses feature-table comparisons.

**Nice to have, judgement call:**
8. Cashback or switching deals. Both Emma and Snoop monetise this, so it is a revenue stream as well as a feature. Needs a partner.
9. Household or family sharing. Emma Ultimate sells extra seats at £5.99, NoReply sells 6 seats for £49/year. People pay for this.
10. Fraud and duplicate-charge detection.
11. Refund expectation tracking. Snoop Plus has the weak version.

**Deliberately do not build:** savings pots with interest, investing, ISAs, pensions, cash advances. These need FCA permissions, capital and a different risk appetite, they put you head-to-head with Plum, Emma and Monzo on their turf, and Cleo's $17m FTC settlement shows how the advance category ends.

### (b) What Paybacker has that none of them have

1. **AI dispute letters citing exact UK legislation, backed by a verified, guardrailed citation database with a founder-approval workflow.** No money app does complaints at all. NoReply does letters but has no verified-citation architecture that it publishes.
2. **End-to-end dispute tracking through provider escalation to the Ombudsman**, with the FCA 8-week clock and an autonomous state machine. Genuinely nobody else.
3. **The dispute outcome dataset** with win rates by merchant and by legal argument, feeding back into letter generation. This compounds and cannot be bought.
4. **Email inbox scanning** for overcharges, forgotten subscriptions, flight-delay opportunities and debt disputes. No money app touches email. No dispute app has any data at all.
5. **MCP integration** so Claude and other AI tools can query your finances. Nobody in UK consumer fintech has this. Lunch Money's API is the closest analogue and it is nowhere near as capable.
6. **A conversational agent on Telegram at every tier**, including free. Cleo is chat-first but locked in its own app and has no dispute capability.
7. **0% success fee.** Claims firms take 25% to 35%. AirHelp-style intermediaries take a similar cut. On a £520 flight claim that is £130 to £180 that Paybacker users keep. This is the single most legible value claim in the whole product and it is currently buried.

### (c) Where Paybacker is genuinely better value

- **Pro at £9.99 versus Emma Pro at £9.99.** Same price, same unlimited connections, plus unlimited AI dispute letters, email scanning, WhatsApp agent, MCP and dispute tracking. If a user values disputes at all, Paybacker Pro is strictly better value than Emma Pro.
- **The stack argument, done honestly.** Emma Pro £9.99 + Snoop Plus £5.99 + NoReply Pro £2.42 = £18.40/month for aggregation, alerts and disputes. Paybacker Pro at £9.99 is about 46% cheaper than that stack. The maths is real.
- **Free tier depth for the dispute job.** 3 AI letters a month free beats NoReply's free tier, which gives analysis but zero written complaints.
- **Annual pricing.** £44.99 and £94.99 are competitive with Emma's discounted annuals.

### (d) Where Paybacker is weaker or at risk

1. **No native app.** Structural. Everything else on this list is downstream of it.
2. **Double consent friction.** Asking an unknown brand for bank OAuth is hard. Asking for full inbox read access on top is much harder. The most differentiating feature is also the highest-friction one, and there is no trust brand behind it (Emma is FCA registered with 3m users, Snoop is owned by a listed bank, Monzo is a bank).
3. **Free tier COGS.** 2 bank connections with daily sync, an email connection with 30-minute polling, and 3 Claude-generated letters a month is a real per-user cost with no forcing function to convert. The initial 2-year inbox backscan is the biggest single unbudgeted AI cost in the product.
4. **NoReply out-executing on the dispute wedge's distribution.** 67 free tools with no signup, per-company pages, complaint-index data pages, a browser extension. That is a compounding organic moat aimed straight at Paybacker's keywords, and Paybacker's own SEO landing pages are still on the to-build list.
5. **Aggregation commoditisation.** Monzo Extra at £3 and Snoop free are collapsing the price of everything in Paybacker's Money Hub. Every engineering hour spent on budgets and categories is an hour spent on a race to zero.
6. **Feature surface too wide for the team.** Budgets, savings goals, net worth, spending intelligence, deals, loyalty points, chatbot, plus disputes, plus a B2B API. The moat is under-invested relative to the commodity.
7. **Regulatory and marketing-claim risk.** The DoNotPay order is the template for how this goes wrong. Any copy implying legal advice or lawyer-equivalence is exposed. The existing guardrail architecture is a genuine defence and should be a published trust asset, not an internal implementation detail.
8. **Emma is about to close the income gap.** The window is measured in weeks, not quarters.

## 6. The pricing question, answered honestly

**The founder's premise:** Paybacker bundles at a fraction of what these cost individually.

**Verdict: arithmetically true, strategically weak, and the tiers are simultaneously under-priced and over-priced.**

**Why the bundle argument is weak.** It only bites if the buyer was going to buy the components. Nobody buys Emma Pro *and* Snoop Plus *and* NoReply Pro. The real alternative in a prospect's head is: Snoop free (aggregation, categorisation, subscription tracking, bill reminders, contract renewal reminders, credit score, weekly report, switching deals) plus Citizens Advice or Resolver templates when something goes wrong, plus Monzo Extra at £3 if they want sheet export. **The competitive reference price for "help me track my money" in the UK in August 2026 is £0 to £3.** Positioning as a cheaper bundle invites comparison on the axis where Paybacker cannot win, and it signals commodity.

**Where the pricing is under-set.** Willingness to pay for disputes is not anchored to budgeting apps, it is anchored to recovery. A flight delay claim is up to £520. An energy billing dispute is routinely £100 to £400. Claims management firms take 25% to 35% of that. NoReply's own homepage does this maths: £220 payout, a claims firm keeps £77, we charge £9.99. Paybacker charges 0% and delivers more (it finds the claim for you, tracks it to the Ombudsman, and grounds the argument in a win-rate dataset). Charging £4.99/month flat for unlimited access to that is leaving a great deal on the table for the subset of users who actually recover money.

**Where the pricing is over-set.** For a user who only wants tracking, £4.99 buys less than Snoop Plus at £5.99 and much less than Snoop free at £0. Essential's headline features (budgets, savings goals, spending dashboard, contract renewal reminders) are all free elsewhere. The price is defensible only once the dispute value is in the frame.

**On AI cost base.** Unlimited letters at £4.99 is survivable. A cited letter with retrieval is single-digit pence of Claude tokens, so even 20 letters a month is well under £2, and the dispute agent's four daily ticks with Haiku extraction are negligible. **The cost that is not survivable is the free tier's 2-year email backscan**, which can be thousands of message classifications for a user who may never pay. That is where margin leaks, not the letters.

**Recommendation.**
- **Do not raise Essential or Pro right now.** You have insufficient conversion data and a price rise adds churn risk without evidence.
- **Change the story, not the number.** Stop selling "cheaper than the others". Sell "we find the money you are owed, we write the letter, we chase it to the Ombudsman, and you keep 100%". Benchmark publicly against claims firms taking 25% to 35%, and against NoReply on capability rather than price.
- **Add a value-capture tier above Pro.** A Dispute Pro at £19.99/month, or a one-off £14.99 Ombudsman escalation pack, or both. Users mid-recovery on a £400 claim will pay this without blinking, and it does not touch the price the tracking-only crowd sees.
- **Add a household plan at ~£14.99.** Emma proves people buy seats (£5.99 each on Ultimate), NoReply proves it at £49/year for six.
- **Cap the free-tier email scan to 90 days** and make "scan 2 years of history" the primary upgrade trigger. This fixes the margin leak and creates a natural conversion event where there currently is none.
- **Push annual harder.** Emma leads with 30% off. Paybacker's £94.99 Pro annual is a 21% discount and is barely surfaced.

## 7. Prioritised roadmap

Ordered by impact divided by effort. **TS** = table stakes (needed to be considered at all). **DIFF** = genuinely differentiating.

### P0, next 4 weeks. The window closes.

| # | Move | Type | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recurring income detection + "What's landing, what's leaving, what's left" forward view** | TS+ | Emma has been asked for this for six years and is still testing it; ship first and you own the copy their own users wrote for you. |
| 2 | **Moneyhub and Money Dashboard migration landing pages** | TS | Moneyhub's app died on 14 August 2026 with data deletion; those users are searching for a home this week and Emma is already courting them. |
| 3 | **Free-tier email scan capped to 90 days, with "scan 2 years" as the upgrade trigger** | n/a | Fixes the single largest unbudgeted AI cost and creates the conversion event the free tier currently lacks. |
| 4 | **Reposition homepage and pricing copy around recovery and 0% fee, not cheaper bundling** | n/a | Copy change only, and it moves the comparison off the axis where Snoop free wins. |

### P1, weeks 4 to 12. Close the credibility gaps.

| # | Move | Type | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **Payday-cycle budgeting periods with bank-holiday shifting** | TS | Emma has had this since 2018 across six cycle types; without it the forward view in P0 is wrong for anyone not paid on a fixed date. |
| 6 | **Installable PWA with push notifications** (native app after) | TS | Cheapest path to the phone; push is also the only reliable way to recover the 90-day open banking re-consent that silently churns every aggregator. |
| 7 | **Manual/offline accounts, manual assets, CSV import, net worth** | TS | The exact bundle Moneyhub refugees need and the exact bundle Emma paywalls at Pro; unblocks a credible net worth number. |
| 8 | **Refund and settlement expectation tracking** ("you were promised £X by date Y, it has not arrived, here is the chaser") | DIFF | Snoop Plus has the weak version; Paybacker owns the dispute thread so it can do the strong version almost free on top of the existing state machine. |
| 9 | **Custom categories and a simple rules engine** | TS | Every paid competitor has it; it is the most common power-user upgrade reason. |

### P2, quarter 4. Build the moat and capture the value.

| # | Move | Type | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **Programmatic dispute SEO: per-company pages, free no-signup calculators, published win-rate data** | DIFF | Directly answers NoReply's 67-tool moat, and Paybacker is the only one that can publish real outcome data rather than opinion. |
| 11 | **Dispute Pro tier and/or one-off Ombudsman escalation pack** | DIFF | Captures value where willingness to pay actually sits, without repricing the tracking crowd. |
| 12 | **Household plan (~£14.99, up to 4 or 6 people)** | TS | Proven purchase behaviour at both Emma and NoReply; pure margin on shared infrastructure. |
| 13 | **Switching and deals affiliate (energy, broadband, insurance)** | n/a | Both Emma and Snoop monetise this; diversifies away from subscription-only revenue. |
| 14 | **Credit score via a bureau partner** | TS | Only to stop losing feature-table comparisons; genuinely low strategic value, do it last or not at all. |

### Explicitly do not build
Savings pots with interest, investing, ISAs, pensions, crypto, cash advances or earned-wage access. Wrong permissions, wrong capital requirements, wrong risk profile, and Cleo's $17m FTC settlement is the cautionary tale for the advance category specifically.

### One standing rule
Every marketing claim about the AI must survive the DoNotPay test. The FTC's finalised order turned on unsubstantiated lawyer-equivalence claims and the absence of testing. Paybacker's citation guardrail and founder-approval workflow are a genuine defence, so publish them as a trust page rather than leaving them as an implementation detail.
