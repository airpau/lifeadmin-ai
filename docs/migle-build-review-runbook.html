How to run the call

Format: you share screen and walk her list 1→11 in her order. Don't reorder to lead with strengths — a reviewer working her own list in sequence gets a cleaner picture, and reordering reads as steering.

  1. Open in 60 seconds, then stop talking. Something like: "I've been through all eleven against the build. Two were partial when I got your document — failed-redirect messaging and transaction pagination — and both are now merged with tests. I'll walk them in your order and you stop me wherever you want." That does three jobs: says you did the work, volunteers what was weak before she asks, and hands her control.
  2. Then just go 1 to 11. The script below has what to click and what to say for each.
  3. Lead each point with the artefact, not the explanation. Show the response, the log line, the test output — then explain. Reviewers trust what they watch happen far more than what they're told.
  4. On points 3 and 11, say they were the gaps. Don't present them as though they were always done. "This one was open when you sent the list — here's the fix and the test that pins it." Volunteering closed gaps buys credibility for the nine you're claiming outright.
  5. When you don't know, say so and write it down. Reviewers are calibrating whether your "yes" means anything. One "I'd have to check" makes the other ten answers worth more.
  6. Close by asking what would fail this. "Is there anything on that list you'd want to see differently before you'd sign it off?" Better to hear it now than in a written follow-up.

Three things to have open but not volunteer unless she asks: the withConsentRetry dead code (point 6), no notification on abandoned consents (point 4), and the four untested areas listed at the bottom. All are honest answers to a direct question; none needs raising unprompted.

SayThe poller runs every minute but won't touch a request until it's three minutes old, so a normal redirect always wins the race. Pagination for transactions

Closed in PR #515
Window
getTransactionsPage sets from, before and limit; legacy to is mirrored onto before
Walk
getAllTransactions honours the server-applied page size over the requested limit, dedupes on (id, date), stops once a page falls below from, 50-page cap
New
Offset continuation. Because before is exclusive, a full page sharing one timestamp left nowhere to advance to and the walk used to stop. It now follows meta.pagination.next.offset within the same window and recovers the rows behind the shared timestamp. Guards: stops if the offset doesn't advance, if the window drops below from, or at the page cap.

SayThis was the second of the two that was open. We window with from and before and walk the pages.
